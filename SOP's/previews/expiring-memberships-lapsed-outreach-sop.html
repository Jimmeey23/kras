1. PURPOSE, SCOPE & OBJECTIVES

1.1 Purpose

This Standard Operating Procedure governs the end-to-end process for membership renewal management and lapsed member re-engagement at Physique 57 India. It establishes a proactive, structured, and relationship-first approach to retaining existing members and reactivating those who have allowed their memberships to lapse.

Renewal and re-engagement are not sales activities in the traditional sense — they are relationship management activities. A client who renews does so because they feel valued, connected to the community, and confident that continuing their membership serves their goals. This SOP ensures that every executive understands how to facilitate that experience consistently.

1.2 Scope

This SOP applies to:

All Sales & Client Servicing Executives responsible for active member accounts

All Team Leaders overseeing sales and servicing teams

The Head of Sales & Client Servicing

All membership types across all Physique 57 India locations — Mumbai and Bengaluru

All members with memberships expiring within 30 days

All members whose memberships have lapsed within the past 12 months

All class-pack holders approaching pack exhaustion

1.3 Why This SOP Exists — The Business Case

THE BUSINESS CASE FOR PROACTIVE RENEWAL MANAGEMENT
Acquiring a new client costs 5–7x more than retaining an existing one.
A lapsed member already knows the brand, the format, the team, and the experience — the barrier to re-entry is far lower than for a cold lead.
Renewing a client is the highest-margin sales activity in any membership business.
Most membership lapse is NOT due to dissatisfaction — it is due to drift, life disruption, or simply not being re-engaged proactively.
A structured, proactive renewal system is one of the single highest-ROI operational investments a studio can make.

1.4 Objectives

Ensure 100% of expiring memberships are identified and actioned at least 30 days before expiry

Deliver a proactive, personalised renewal experience that does not feel like a sales call

Reduce unintentional membership lapse through early and consistent outreach

Re-engage lapsed members through a structured, empathetic outreach protocol

Identify at-risk members before they lapse through attendance monitoring

Maintain clean CRM records for all active, expiring, and lapsed memberships

Drive upgrade conversations where appropriate to longer-term or higher-value plans

3. THE RENEWAL & RETENTION PHILOSOPHY

Physique 57's renewal and re-engagement approach is built on three foundational principles. Every executive must internalise these principles — they are not marketing language; they are operational guidelines.

PRINCIPLEWHAT IT MEANS IN PRACTICE
Proactive, Not ReactiveWe reach out to members before they lapse — not after. Waiting for a member to notice their membership has expired and contact us is a failure of the system. Our role is to be one step ahead at all times.
Relationship-First, Transaction-SecondRenewal conversations must always begin with genuine interest in the member's experience, progress, and goals. The commercial ask comes after the connection has been re-established. Members who feel like a sale will resist. Members who feel valued will renew.
Personalisation Over TemplatesEvery renewal message should feel like it was written specifically for that person. Referencing their class history, their goals, their favourite format, or their instructor creates the emotional resonance that drives renewal decisions.
THE MOST IMPORTANT INSIGHT IN THIS SOP
The single most common reason members do not renew is not cost — it is that no one asked them to.
In our business, silence communicates indifference. Proactive, warm outreach communicates community.
Every member who leaves without being contacted represents both a revenue loss and a relationship failure.

7. UPGRADE & LOYALTY PROTOCOL

7.1 When to Introduce an Upgrade

An upgrade should be introduced naturally during the renewal conversation — never as an opening gambit. The right moment is after the CARE framework Connect and Acknowledge steps, once rapport has been re-established. An upgrade conversation must feel like the executive is acting in the member's best interest, not upselling for the sake of it.

Introduce an upgrade when the following conditions are met:

Member's attendance data supports a higher frequency plan (more classes per week than their current plan optimally covers)

Member has been on the same plan for 2+ renewal cycles — a longer commitment may be more economical

Member has expressed fitness goals that would benefit from more consistent practice

A price-saving calculation genuinely favours the longer-term plan

7.2 How to Present an Upgrade

Always lead with the savings — show the per-session or per-month cost comparison concretely

Frame it as the member investing in themselves, not Physique 57 getting more revenue

Example: 'You've been coming 4 times a week — which means on your current plan you're paying about ₹X per session. The annual plan brings that down to ₹Y per session. It pays for itself within the first 4 months.'

Do not push an upgrade if the member has expressed cost concerns — offer the renewal at their current tier first

7.3 Loyalty Recognition

Long-standing members represent the heart of the Physique 57 community. Their renewal deserves acknowledgement beyond a transactional exchange. The following loyalty touchpoints should be incorporated into renewal conversations:

MILESTONERECOGNITION ACTION
Member renewing for the 2nd timeAcknowledge their commitment. 'This is your second year with us — we're so glad you're part of the community.'
Member renewing for the 3rd+ timePersonalised note from the Team Leader or Head of Sales. Mention their loyalty explicitly.
Annual membership renewalExpress genuine appreciation for the level of commitment. Mention how valued they are to the community.
Member crossing 100 classesFlag in CRM. Celebrate via WhatsApp or in-studio. Use as a renewal hook — 'You're at 97 classes — you'll hit 100 soon!'
Member celebrating 1 yearAcknowledge the anniversary. 'It's been a whole year with us — you should be incredibly proud.'

8. RENEWAL OBJECTION HANDLING

Renewal objections are fundamentally different from new-lead objections. A renewing member has already validated the product. Their hesitation is almost always circumstantial — financial, logistical, or life-related — rather than experiential. Approach with empathy, not persuasion.

OBJECTIONUNDERLYING CONCERNRECOMMENDED RESPONSE APPROACH
'I'll renew next month'Procrastination. Or a real financial timing issue.Ask if there's a specific reason for the delay. If financial: offer a shorter plan now to bridge the gap. If procrastination: offer to process immediately to save them the hassle. Remind them that a gap in their practice is harder to restart from.
'I've been travelling / busy / not attending much'Guilt about not using their membership. May feel renewal is a waste.Validate without dismissing. Ask when they expect to be back. Offer a freeze extension option if the lapse was legitimate. Remind them that life happens and the studio will be there when they're ready.
'It's quite expensive right now'Genuine budget pressure or perceived value gap.Acknowledge without apologising for pricing. Offer a shorter plan to reduce the upfront commitment. Show per-session cost. Explore installment options if available.
'I want to try other studios'Curiosity or dissatisfaction. Explore which.Ask what's prompting the curiosity — is it something new they want to try, or something missing here? If it's a specific format or feature — address it. Do not dismiss competitor interest; understand it.
'I'm not sure I'll use it consistently'Confidence / commitment anxiety.Offer a shorter plan (1 month or class pack) to re-establish the habit. Remove the long-term commitment pressure. Focus on the next 4 weeks, not the next 6 months.
'I want to take a break for a month or two'Genuine desire for a pause — not necessarily a churn signal.Explore whether a freeze option covers their need. If yes — process the freeze. If no — offer a minimal bridge plan. Do not push renewal on a member who genuinely needs a break.
'I'll think about it and get back to you'Classic delay. Unresolved concern underneath.Ask what specifically needs thinking through. Often reveals the real objection. Offer to send an options summary. Set a specific follow-up time — 'Shall I check back with you on [specific date]?'

All approved discounts must be logged in CRM with authorising manager's name and reason.

10.2 Renewal That Lapses Before Processing

If a member's membership expires before the renewal is processed (i.e. they agree to renew after expiry), the following applies:

Do not extend the original membership retroactively unless approved by the Team Leader

The new membership begins from the date of payment processing, not the original expiry date

Any gap in attendance due to the lapse is noted in CRM — the member is not penalised

If the member had booked classes during the lapsed period, verify with the Team Leader how those bookings are handled

13.2 REASON-DISSATISFIED Members — Special Protocol

DISSATISFIED LAPSED MEMBERS — MANDATORY ESCALATION BEFORE OUTREACH
Members who lapsed because of dissatisfaction MUST NOT be re-engaged by a standard executive without Team Leader review.
Before any outreach to a REASON-DISSATISFIED member: the Team Leader must review the complaint history, resolution status, and whether outreach is appropriate.
If the complaint was unresolved: resolve it first. Do not attempt a commercial re-engagement before the relationship has been repaired.
If the complaint was resolved: the Team Leader may authorise outreach with a specific approach brief.
A REASON-DISSATISFIED member who returns after a resolved issue is one of the strongest community stories — handle with corresponding care.

14. WIN-BACK OFFERS & INCENTIVE GUIDELINES

Win-back offers are structured incentives designed to lower the barrier to re-entry for extended or dormant lapsed members. They are not standard discounts — they are deliberate investment in client reactivation. All win-back offers must be pre-approved as per the Discount & Incentive Authority (Section 9).

14.1 Win-Back Offer Design Principles

A win-back offer must feel like a genuine gesture of welcome — not a clearance sale

The offer should create a reason to return now, not 'whenever you're ready'

Win-back offers should be time-bounded — open-ended offers lose urgency and are rarely acted upon

The offer tier should match the lapse duration — a longer-lapsed member may need a more generous incentive

Win-back offers must never be communicated before the relationship conversation (Section 13) has re-established warmth

15. ATTENDANCE-BASED RETENTION MONITORING

The most powerful retention tool is early intervention — identifying at-risk members before they make the conscious decision to lapse. Attendance data is the most reliable leading indicator of imminent churn. The following monitoring protocol applies to all active members.

15.2 What the Retention Check-In Is NOT

THE PURPOSE OF AN ATTENDANCE CHECK-IN
A retention check-in is NOT a sales call. It is a relationship call.
Do not mention membership expiry, renewal, or pricing during a 7-day or 14-day absence check-in.
The goal is to understand why the member has been away and to make them feel that the studio noticed and cares.
The commercial conversation — if needed — comes only after you understand the situation.
A member who feels genuinely cared for is far more likely to renew than one who feels chased.

15.3 Attendance Data Review Responsibility

Executives must review their assigned active member attendance report every Monday morning

Team Leaders must review team-wide attendance flags every Monday and Wednesday

Any member crossing an absence threshold that was not actioned within 24 hours is a compliance gap

Attendance flags must be logged in CRM as a touchpoint — even 'no action needed' check-ins
