1. PURPOSE & THE SALES PROBLEM WE ARE SOLVING

The current sales approach at Physique 57 has a critical flaw: executives are pitching 3-month, 6-month, and annual memberships as a default because those packages carry the highest ticket value — not because they are the right fit for every client.

This creates two problems:

Clients who are genuinely suited to higher-value packages are not being converted because the pitch is not personalised — it feels like a product push, not a recommendation

Clients who would thrive on a class pack or short membership are being pitched long-term commitments they are not ready for, which leads to objections, hesitation, and no sale at all

THE FIXThe job of the executive is not to sell the highest-value package. It is to recommend the most suitable package — and then communicate its value so clearly that the client wants to say yes. The right recommendation, delivered well, will close at a higher value than a mis-matched expensive pitch delivered poorly.

2. THE DISCOVERY FRAMEWORK — BEFORE YOU PITCH

Before recommending any membership, an executive must understand the client. This requires a structured conversation — not a form, not a checklist read aloud, but a genuine discovery exchange that the client experiences as being understood, not being sold to.

The Discovery Framework consists of 5 questions. These should flow naturally within the conversation — not be fired sequentially like an interview.

The 5 Discovery Questions

#QuestionWhat You Are Really Finding Out
Q1How many days a week are you realistically able to come in?Frequency commitment. This determines whether a class pack or unlimited membership is appropriate. 2 days = class pack. 4+ days = unlimited is better value.
Q2What is your primary goal — are you looking to build strength, lose weight, improve flexibility, or just feel better overall?Motivation type. Fitness-goal-oriented clients tend to be more committed and suit longer memberships. Social/wellbeing clients may prefer flexibility.
Q3Have you tried this kind of training before, or would this be your first experience with it?Experience level. First-timers benefit from starting with a trial + short membership (2-week or 1-month) before committing longer. Experienced movers are ready to commit faster.
Q4How long have you been thinking about joining? Is there a specific reason you are ready to start now?Urgency and readiness. A client who says 'I have been thinking for 6 months' needs a confidence nudge — not an annual membership pitch that triggers another 6 months of deliberation.
Q5Is there anything about timings, pricing, or format that you would like me to walk you through before we look at options?Objection pre-emption. Surfaces concerns before they become blockers. Gives you the chance to resolve them before the pitch.
TECHNIQUEAfter asking Q1, always LISTEN to the full answer before speaking. The most common mistake is asking 'how many days a week can you come?' and then immediately pitching the 3-month unlimited before the client has finished their sentence.

5. THE PITCH FRAMEWORK — HOW TO PRESENT A MEMBERSHIP

Once you have completed the Discovery Framework and identified the right membership category, the pitch itself should follow a structured 4-step framework. This ensures the recommendation feels like advice — not a sales push.

Step 1 — Mirror Back (Show You Were Listening)

Before presenting any option, briefly reflect what the client told you. This builds trust and confirms you understood them correctly.

EXAMPLE'So based on what you have shared — you are looking to come in about 3 times a week, you have done some Barre before, and you want to feel stronger and leaner in the next few months. Does that sound right?'

Step 2 — Present One Option (Not Three)

The most common sales mistake in boutique fitness is presenting every package and letting the client pick. This creates confusion, invites comparison, and triggers price-shock.

Present ONE recommended option first. Frame it as your recommendation, not a menu:

EXAMPLE'For where you are right now — 3 times a week, clear goal, ready to commit — I would recommend the 3-month unlimited. It gives you 90 days of unlimited access across all formats, and that is genuinely where the results start to show. At your frequency, it works out to well under INR 2,000 per session.'

Step 3 — Anchor on Value, Not Price

Do not lead with the number. Lead with what they get. The price is context for the value — not the other way around.

Translate the price into per-class terms: 'At 3x per week, that works out to roughly INR 1,900 per session.'

Compare to their current context: 'That is less than a single personal training session at a gym, with unlimited access.'

Connect to their goal: 'For the kind of results you are describing, consistency over 3 months is what actually works. This membership supports exactly that.'

Step 4 — Handle the Objection Calmly, Then Offer the Alternative

If the client hesitates on the primary recommendation, do not immediately drop to a lower package. First, understand the hesitation:

ObjectionResponse
'The price is a bit high for me right now.''I completely understand. Can I ask — is it the total amount that is the concern, or is it more about the monthly commitment? If it is the total, we do have a monthly installment option for the 3-month plan at INR 15,225 per month — same unlimited access, spread across three payments.'
'I am not sure I will be able to come that often.''That is really honest — I appreciate it. If 3 times a week feels uncertain, then honestly the 10-class pack might be a smarter starting point for you. 10 classes, 70 days of validity, no pressure on frequency. And when you find your rhythm, upgrading is easy.'
'I want to try it more before committing.''Absolutely. The 1-month unlimited is a great way to really test it out across different formats and timings before you decide on a longer plan. It is INR 18,638 and gives you unlimited access for 30 days — then you have everything you need to decide what feels right.'
'Let me think about it.''Of course! Can I ask — is there anything specific you would like me to clarify or walk you through? Sometimes there is one thing that makes the decision easy once it is clear.' [If no — set a specific follow-up: 'I will follow up with you on Wednesday — does that work?']

7. WHAT NOT TO DO — COMMON SALES MISTAKES

MistakeWhy It Fails
Pitching 3M/6M/Annual to every client as the defaultMis-matched pitch triggers objections that did not need to exist. A client who would have happily bought a 1-month membership is now resistant because they felt pressured.
Asking 'how many classes per week' then ignoring the answerThe discovery question becomes meaningless. Client notices. Trust erodes.
Presenting the entire pricing menu upfrontCreates comparison paralysis. The client compares prices and picks the cheapest — or picks nothing.
Saying 'this is our best package' without contextEmpty statement. Clients hear this as a sales line, not advice. Always connect the recommendation to what they told you.
Dropping to a lower package at the first sign of hesitationSignals that you did not believe in your own recommendation. Understand the objection first, then respond appropriately.
Pitching Strength Lab to a client who is at Bandra or BLRStrength Lab is only at Kwality House. Pitching an unavailable format destroys credibility.
Not mentioning per-class value at allHigh upfront numbers feel large without context. Per-class breakdown makes the value tangible and comparable.
