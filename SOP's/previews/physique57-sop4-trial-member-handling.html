1. PURPOSE & SCOPE

This SOP defines the complete handling process for trial members at Physique 57 India — from the moment a trial is booked to the post-trial follow-up cadence. The trial is the most powerful conversion tool in P57's sales arsenal. Every element of the trial experience must be orchestrated, not left to chance.

All Sales & Client Servicing Executives responsible for trial bookings

Studio floor teams and instructors on trial class days

Team Leaders responsible for trial day oversight

All locations: Kwality House, Supreme HQ, Kenkere House, Copper N Cloves (pop-up)

2. WHAT A TRIAL REPRESENTS — THE BRAND LENS

THE TRIAL IS THE PRODUCT. TREAT IT ACCORDINGLY.
A trial is not a free class. It is a curated first experience of the Physique 57 brand.
The prospect arrives with hope, curiosity, and a degree of vulnerability. They are trusting us with their first impression of something new.
Every person on the floor — the executive, the instructor, the front desk — is performing in that first impression.
A trial member who leaves feeling welcomed, capable, and inspired will convert. One who leaves feeling ignored, overwhelmed, or awkward will not — regardless of how good the class was.
We do not get a second chance at a first experience.

4.4 Studio Readiness Checklist — Trial Day

On the day of a scheduled trial, the executive on duty must complete the following by 30 minutes before the class:

Confirm trial attendee list with class register or booking system

Brief the instructor: trial attendee name, any concerns flagged (injuries, first-timers, etc.)

Ensure front desk knows to greet the trial member by name upon arrival

Confirm grip socks are available if Barre trial (available to purchase or provide if policy allows)

Ensure the trial member has a reserved spot in class if applicable

5. TRIAL DAY — ARRIVAL & WELCOME PROTOCOL

The arrival experience sets the emotional tone for everything that follows — including the conversion conversation. It must be warm, personal, and effortless for the trial member.

5.2 Arrival Standards — Non-Negotiable

A trial member must NEVER arrive and have to introduce themselves unprompted

A trial member must NEVER be left standing at the front desk without being acknowledged within 60 seconds

A trial member must NEVER be made to feel like a disruption during a busy class changeover

The experience of the first 5 minutes at the studio is as important as the 45 minutes of class

6. DURING THE TRIAL — STUDIO FLOOR STANDARDS

The executive's role during a trial class is light-touch observation, not intervention. The instructor owns the class experience. However, the executive should:

Remain aware of the trial member's experience — visible but not hovering

Note any visible engagement signals (enjoying the class, struggling, connecting with others)

Be ready to step in if the member appears lost, uncomfortable, or needs assistance with equipment

Not approach the member mid-class for any non-safety reason

6.1 Instructor Responsibilities During Trial Classes

Instructors must be briefed by the executive before every class with a trial attendee. Instructor responsibilities include:

Giving the trial member a warm, specific shout-out at the start (optional but powerful): 'We have some new faces joining us today — welcome!'

Offering appropriate modifications for the first-time format experience

Not singling out the trial member in a way that could feel embarrassing

Checking in with the trial member during any water break if class format allows

Delivering the same high-energy, high-quality class regardless of how many trials are present

7. POST-CLASS — THE CONVERSION WINDOW

THE POST-CLASS WINDOW — USE IT WISELY
The 10 minutes after a trial class is the highest-value sales window in the entire customer journey.
The prospect's body is alive, their endorphins are high, and their emotional connection to the experience is at its peak.
This is not the time to pitch. This is the time to listen, celebrate, and guide — in that order.
A premature or clumsy commercial ask at this moment can undo everything the class just built.

9. NO-SHOW & CANCELLATION PROTOCOL

9.1 No-Show Protocol

STEPACTIONOWNERSLA
1Class ends. Trial member did not attend. Log as NO-SHOW in CRM within 1 hour.ExecutiveWithin 1 hour of class end
2Send warm, non-accusatory check-in WhatsApp: 'Hey [Name]! Missed you in class today — hope everything is okay. No worries at all! Would love to find another time that works for you.'ExecutiveWithin 2 hours of class end
3Attempt a call the following morning if no WhatsApp response.ExecutiveNext morning by 11 AM
4If response within 48 hours: rebook immediately. Offer 2–3 specific slots.ExecutiveWithin same conversation
5If no response within 7 days of no-show: reclassify as COLD. Standard nurture cadence applies.ExecutiveDay 7 post no-show
6After 2 no-shows for the same lead: flag to Team Leader before any further outreach.ExecutiveAfter 2nd no-show

9.2 Cancellation Protocol

Acknowledge cancellation graciously — never express disappointment

Immediately offer 2–3 specific rebooking slots — secure the rebook in the same message

Update CRM: mark original booking as CANCELLED, create new booking record

Maximum 2 rescheduling attempts before moving to COLD tier

A cancellation with advance notice is very different from a same-day cancellation or no-show — treat accordingly

10. MULTI-FORMAT TRIAL HANDLING

Some prospects wish to trial multiple formats before committing. This is a positive buying signal — they are seriously considering a Studio multi-format membership. The handling requires specific coordination.

Multi-format interest must be logged in CRM at point of discovery

Sequence format trials based on the prospect's stated primary goal (e.g. if goal is strength — start with Strength Lab, follow with Barre)

Space trials no more than 5–7 days apart — extended gaps between trials reduce conversion momentum

Use the inter-trial period for relationship building, not aggressive pitching

After the second trial: present the Studio multi-format unlimited as the natural recommendation

Do not offer a third trial before a conversion conversation — if two trials haven't triggered a decision, explore the real barrier
