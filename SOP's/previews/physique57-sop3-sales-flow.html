1. PURPOSE, SCOPE & OBJECTIVES

1.1 Purpose

This SOP defines the complete, end-to-end sales process for Physique 57 India — from the moment a lead enters the system to the moment a membership is activated. It removes ambiguity, standardises every stage of the sales journey, and ensures every prospective member receives a consistent, high-quality, brand-appropriate sales experience regardless of which executive handles the interaction.

1.2 Scope

All Sales & Client Servicing Executives across all P57 India locations

All Team Leaders managing sales teams

All lead types: performance marketing, organic, referral, walk-in, influencer, event

All formats: Barre, PowerCycle, Strength Lab, Studio (multi-format), Private, Virtual Private

1.3 Objectives

Standardise sales behaviour across all locations and executives

Reduce lead leakage at every stage of the funnel

Improve trial-to-sale and lead-to-trial conversion rates

Ensure package recommendations are consultative, personalised, and accurate

Eliminate unauthorised discounting and pricing inconsistency

Give management full visibility into pipeline health and individual performance

2. THE PHYSIQUE 57 SALES PHILOSOPHY

THE P57 SALES PHILOSOPHY — EVERY EXECUTIVE MUST INTERNALISE THIS
We do not sell memberships. We invite people into a life-changing practice.
The goal of every sales conversation is to understand what the prospect needs — and then show them how Physique 57 serves that need.
A sale made by listening and guiding lasts longer, renews more often, and refers more clients than a sale made by pushing.
Price is never the real objection. Connection, confidence, and clarity are what close a sale.
We do not chase. We follow up — consistently, warmly, and with purpose.

Physique 57's brand is premium, community-driven, and transformation-focused. Every interaction in the sales process must reflect that positioning. An executive who rushes, pressures, or over-discounts does not just lose a sale — they damage the brand.

5. STAGE 2 — FIRST CONTACT & DISCOVERY

5.1 First Contact SLA

Refer to SOP 1.0 Section 7 for channel-specific SLA table. Core rule: performance marketing leads within 15 minutes, WhatsApp leads within 10 minutes, referrals within 1 hour, walk-ins immediately.

5.2 Discovery — The Foundation of Every Sale

Discovery is the most important stage in the sales process. An executive who skips discovery and jumps to pricing is making a fundamental error. Discovery achieves three things: it builds trust, it uncovers the right package recommendation, and it gives the prospect the experience of being heard — which is itself a differentiator.

The following discovery areas must be covered in first contact — conversationally, not as an interrogation:

DISCOVERY AREAQUESTION TO ASK (ADAPT NATURALLY)WHY IT MATTERS
Fitness GoalWhat are you hoping to achieve — strength, flexibility, cardio, weight management, or something else?Determines format recommendation and emotional hook
Format FamiliarityHave you tried Barre, PowerCycle, or Strength Lab before — or would this be a first?Sets expectations; prevents mismatch; enables expert guidance
Location & ConvenienceWhich studio is most convenient for you — Kwality House, Supreme HQ, or Kenkere House?Prevents booking error; identifies Strength Lab eligibility
Schedule AvailabilityWhat days and times typically work for you — mornings, evenings, weekends?Enables class slot matching; reduces no-show risk
Commitment ReadinessAre you looking to start soon, or still exploring your options?Qualifies tier — Hot / Warm / Cold — determines follow-up urgency
Budget Comfort (indirect)Are you looking for something flexible month-to-month, or happy to commit for longer if the value is there?Surfaces budget sensitivity without asking directly about price
Prior Experience / ConcernsHave you worked out in a boutique studio setting before? Any injuries or things I should know?Safety flag; onboarding prep; shows care beyond the sale

5.3 Discovery Outcome

By the end of first contact, the executive must know enough to:

Classify the lead tier (Hot / Warm / Cold / Nurture)

Know which format to recommend for their trial

Know which studio location to direct them to

Have offered a specific trial booking slot

Have logged all discovery findings in CRM notes

The 10 minutes before and after a trial class are some of the highest-value sales minutes in the entire process.
A lead who feels personally welcomed, well-cared-for, and genuinely seen during their trial is 3x more likely to convert than one who simply attended a class.
The studio floor is a sales environment. Every person on it is part of the sales team during a trial.

7. STAGE 4 — POST-TRIAL CONVERSION

Refer to SOP 1.0 Section 8.3 for the full post-trial follow-up cadence (6 touchpoints over 14 days). The conversion strategy guidelines are below.

7.1 The Golden Window

THE 48-HOUR RULE
The 24–48 hours after a trial class is the highest-conversion window in the entire sales cycle.
A prospect's emotional connection to the experience is at its peak immediately after the class.
Every hour of delay in post-trial outreach reduces the conversion probability.
Same-day WhatsApp + Day 1 call are mandatory — not optional.

7.2 Post-Trial Conversation Goals

Post-trial outreach has a specific sequence of goals — do not jump to the close before the earlier steps are complete:

TOUCHPOINTPRIMARY GOALWHAT NOT TO DO

Day 2 MessageReinforce the value. Share a specific insight relevant to their goal. Recommend a package.Do not repeat the same message. Add new value.

8. STAGE 5 — PACKAGE PRESENTATION & CLOSE

8.1 The Recommendation-First Approach

Executives must NEVER present the full membership menu to a prospect. A menu creates confusion, comparison paralysis, and price-shopping. Instead: listen during discovery, then make one clear, well-reasoned recommendation.

The recommendation must be:

Based on the prospect's stated goals, schedule, and budget comfort signals

Explained with a specific rationale: 'Based on what you've told me about [X], I'd recommend [Y] because [Z]'

Presented with the per-session or per-month cost, not just the total

Followed by one upgrade option only — if relevant

8.3 Closing Language

The close must be a forward-moving question — not a passive invitation:

CORRECT: 'Based on what you've told me, I think the 3-month plan is the perfect fit for you. Shall I get that set up for you today?'

INCORRECT: 'So, are you interested in joining?' (binary — invites a no)

INCORRECT: 'Let me know whenever you're ready.' (no call to action — defers indefinitely)

CORRECT: 'I can have your membership active and your first class booked within the next 10 minutes — would that work for you?'

10. OBJECTION HANDLING MASTER GUIDE

Objections are not rejections. They are requests for more information, more reassurance, or more time. Every objection has an underlying concern — the executive's job is to find it and address it.

OBJECTIONUNDERLYING CONCERNRESPONSE APPROACH
'It's too expensive'Value not yet established. Or genuine budget constraint.Reframe per-session cost. Compare to alternatives contextually. Offer a shorter commitment to lower entry point. Explore instalment option.
'I need to think about it'Unresolved concern — usually one specific thing.Ask: 'Of course — is there something specific I can help you think through?' Almost always reveals the real objection.
'I'll start next month'Procrastination or genuine timing issue.Ask what changes next month. If nothing — offer a bridge (1-month or small pack). Remind them: starting is harder than continuing.
'I need to check my schedule'Commitment anxiety or schedule genuinely uncertain.Help them check it now. Pull up the class schedule. Offer flexible packs if schedule is the real barrier.
'I'm not sure I'll come regularly'Fear of wasting money. Habit uncertainty.Validate the concern. Start small — 1-month or 8-class pack. Remove the long-term commitment. Focus on the next 30 days.
'Can I get a discount?'Negotiation habit or genuine price sensitivity.Never discount unilaterally. Say: 'Let me check what we have available and come back to you.' Consult Team Leader.
'I want to try one more class first'Hasn't fully committed emotionally yet.Offer a multi-class introductory pack (4-class or Newcomers 2-for-1). Frame it as the exploration phase before the commitment.
'My friend goes to [competitor]'Social influence. Possible comparison shopping.Acknowledge without disparaging. Ask what's most important to them in a studio. Position P57's specific differentiators against that priority.
'I'm travelling / life is busy'Genuine disruption or avoidance mechanism.If genuine — offer flexible pack. If avoidance — ask when things settle and set a follow-up for that specific date.

12. SALES GOVERNANCE & NON-NEGOTIABLE RULES

SALES GOVERNANCE — ABSOLUTE RULES — ALL APPLY AT ALL TIMES
RULE 1: No executive may offer or confirm a discount without Team Leader approval — no exceptions.
RULE 2: All leads must be in the CRM with a named owner before any outreach is made.
RULE 3: No executive may make pricing commitments that differ from the current approved pricing without written authorisation.
RULE 4: Cash payments require dual sign-off and same-day accounts logging.
RULE 5: No executive may poach or contact a lead assigned to another executive without Team Leader authorisation.
RULE 6: All follow-up must stop immediately when a lead or member requests DNC — this must be logged in CRM within 10 minutes.
RULE 7: Membership activation must never precede payment confirmation.

13. DAILY SALES RHYTHM & TIME BLOCKS

Given that executives manage both sales and client servicing simultaneously, a structured daily rhythm is essential to protect sales activity from being consumed by reactive client queries. The following time-blocking framework is recommended.

TIME BLOCKACTIVITYPRIORITY
7:00 – 9:00 AMMorning class coverage. Walk-in greetings. Trial attendee management. Check-ins.Operational — Studio First
9:00 – 10:00 AMCRM review: expiry flags, overdue follow-ups, new leads from overnight. Plan the day.Planning — Non-Negotiable
10:00 – 12:00 PMSALES BLOCK: Outbound calls + WhatsApp follow-ups. Lead outreach. Post-trial cadence.Revenue — Protect This Block
12:00 – 5:30 PMClient query management, walk-in support, booking assistance, studio coordination.Client Servicing — Responsive
5:30 – 6:30 PMSALES BLOCK 2: Evening follow-ups. Leads who couldn't be reached in morning.Revenue — Second Sales Window
6:30 – 8:00 PMEvening class coverage. End-of-day CRM update. Daily sales report submission.Operational + Admin
PROTECTING THE SALES BLOCK
The 10:00 AM – 12:00 PM block is the primary protected sales window. Client queries that are not urgent must wait.
Team Leaders must enforce this time block — executives pulled into non-sales work during this window underperform on conversion targets.
Urgency vs. importance: a client question about class schedule is important but not urgent. A Hot lead follow-up is both.
